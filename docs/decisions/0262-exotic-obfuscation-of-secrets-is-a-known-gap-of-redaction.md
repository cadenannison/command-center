# 0262. Exotic obfuscation of a secret is a known gap of redaction

Status: accepted

Context: Reviews of R1 kept finding Unicode tricks that hide a number or a key from the matchers. Decision 0240 already says redaction is best-effort; the project manager asked for the exotic cases to be recorded as a known gap and not chased with more code. Text reaches `redact` from the user's own calendars, Canvas and mail subjects, so deliberate evasion is not the threat it defends against; accidental secrets in ordinary text are.
Decision: These are known gaps, with no code. (1) Combining marks outside U+0300 to U+036F between the digits of a number or the letters of a keyword: the enclosing keycap U+20E3 (keycap emoji digits such as `8️⃣0️⃣1️⃣`), the enclosing circle U+20DD, and the blocks U+1AB0 to U+1AFF, U+1DC0 to U+1DFF, U+20D0 to U+20FF and U+FE20 to U+FE2F. U+0300 to U+036F is covered (0243), so an underlined number is masked, but `PIN 4̲8̲2̲1̲` (a 4 digit code with marks) and an underlined keyword (`P̲I̲N̲ 4821`) are not. (2) Characters that render blank but are not default-ignorable and not whitespace: the Braille blank U+2800, the interlinear annotation marks U+FFF9 to U+FFFB and the Egyptian hieroglyph format controls U+13430 to U+1343F, used between the digits of a number. (3) Homoglyphs that NFKC does not fold: Cyrillic or Greek letters inside a key prefix, inside `code` or `pin`, or inside `[at]`. (4) Any mix of the above, and any other deliberate evasion: digits spelled as words, a secret reversed or encoded, a number split across lines with text between.
Alternative: Strip every combining mark (category Mn and Me) that follows a digit or a letter before matching (rejected for now: general and linear, but it would also change accented text sent onward, `e` plus U+0301 is composed by NFKC, a mark on its own is not, and a catalogue of what real text needs has not been made). Add the missing ranges one by one (rejected: the list can never be complete, and each addition is a rule that needs tests and a record).
Reason: Realistic secret-bearing text (emails, tokens and keys, URLs with tokens, one-time codes, phone numbers, IP addresses) is covered and property-tested; the cost of the next trick is not worth a rule each, and 0240 states that callers still choose what goes in (titles, due dates, trimmed subjects; never bodies).
Consequences: A person who types a phone number in keycap emoji or hides a PIN with combining marks leaks it to the provider when the AI summary is on. The summary is opt-in and off by default (AGENTS.md rule 6), and the item text is a title or a trimmed subject.
Where: `docs/contracts/core.md` (Redaction, known gaps), `src/command_center/core/redact.py` (`_INVISIBLE`, `_SEPARATORS`, `_CODE_GAP` hold the covered sets).
Revisit when: A leak of one of these classes is seen in real text (not a constructed example), or the combining-mark pre-pass above is wanted for another reason.
