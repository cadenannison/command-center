# 0280. Redaction keeps dates, times, course codes, versions, grades, file names and word phrases

Status: accepted; supersedes in part 0241 (a token run made of words joined by "_" or "-"), 0243 (the digit-group exemption beyond ISO dates; slash dates are no longer a known false positive) and 0244 (a four-part version after a version word)

Context: An independent review ran `redact` on 135 realistic agenda lines and found it damaging ordinary text: `Exam 11/12 10:30 AM` and `Dentist appointment 11/12/2026 3:00 PM` lost their date and time to the digit-group rule (a date, a space and an hour are 6+ digits), `CS 2420-001`, `Semester 2026-2027`, `FY 2026/27` and `GPA 3.85/4.00` were read as phone numbers, `Release 2.4.1.7` as an IP address, and `Essay_Draft_v2_FINAL_final.docx` and `Internationalization_and_Localization_Review` as keys. On the 43 lines of the new agenda corpus (13 the card named, 30 more), 26 changed before this decision and none do after. The project manager's priority rule: when a leak and a false positive conflict, keep dates, times, course codes, versions, grades and file names; a readable summary matters more than catching exotic secrets.
Decision: (1) Inside a digit group these stay, each bounded by whitespace or the edge of the group (the rule 0011 and 0243 already use for ISO dates): an ISO date or range; M/D, M/D/YYYY and M/D/YY with a month and day of 1-31, and ranges of them joined by a dash (`11/20-11/22`); `YYYY-YYYY`, `YYYY-YY` and `YYYY/YY` (years 19xx and 20xx); a section range `15.1-15.4`; a grade `N.NN/N.NN` or `92.5/100` (a decimal numerator, so `100/100` and `801/555 1234` are not exempt); and a course number `NNNN-NNN` or `NNNN/NNNN` right after a letters-only prefix of 2-6 letters (`CS 2420-001`, `PHYS 2210/2215`, `CS2420-001`). The rest of the group is judged on its own, so `12/12/2026 8015551234` gives `12/12/2026 [number]`. (2) A separator never joins a digit to a following `H:MM` time: `2026 10:30` and `801 555 1234 3:00 PM` are two groups. (3) A dotted quad right after `v`, `ver`, `version`, `release`, `rel`, `build`, `rev` or `revision` (case-insensitive, at most two of `space tab : #` between) is a version and stays, both in the digit rule and in the IPv4 rule; any other dotted quad is an address (`10.0.0.1`, `192.168.1.20`, `IP 1.2.3.4`). (4) A token run is not a token when it is a phrase: after dropping edge `_` and `-`, it splits at `_` and `-` into 2 or more pieces, every piece is a word (2+ letters, at most two lower-to-upper case flips) or a number (`[A-Za-z]?` and 1-8 digits: `v2`, `Q4`, `2026`), and at least two pieces are words of 3+ letters. The rule applies to each part of the 24+ digit rule and to the whole-run base64 rule. A key with separators and a mixed piece (`550e8400-e29b-41d4-...`), a mixed-case piece with many flips, or no real words (`ab-ab-ab-...`) is still a token, and known-prefix keys are masked by their own rules first. (5) The token-run rule runs a second time after the IPv4 sweep, because a dotted address inside a run (`PM2.4.1.7`) stopped the run rule on the first pass and not on the second. (6) A forward or backward code match does not mask a 4-digit number that follows an all-capitals word of 2-6 letters that is not itself the keyword (`Verify PSY 1010`, `CS 2420 code review`), see 0281.
Alternative: Keep the digit rule as it was and exempt only the lines the review listed (rejected: the same shapes appear with other numbers). Exempt every `N/N` pair (rejected: `801/555 1234` would leak a whole phone number). Decide a version by its octet values (rejected: `2.4.1.7` and `10.0.0.1` cannot be told apart; a version word is the only signal in the text). Judge a key by the longest piece between separators (the card's example; rejected: a real key has one long piece and a phrase has short ones only when the words are short, `Internationalization` is 20 letters, so the piece kinds, not their length, decide). Leave the 32-character rule alone and only raise it (rejected: `Essay_Draft_v2_FINAL_final` is 26 characters and holds a digit, so the 24-with-digit rule masked it too).
Reason: A summary of `[number] [number] [number]` is useless, and none of these shapes is a secret. The exemptions are the narrowest forms that cover the review's lines: each needs a boundary, a plausible range, or a prefix.
Consequences: Known false positives left on purpose: `100/100` and any `N/N` score with 6+ digits; `Code Camp 2026`, `Room 2031 code review` and similar (a year or room number within five words of a keyword is masked, as in 0242); `Invoice INV-2026-00123` and `pp. 1234-1250` (8 and 9 digits, no exemption); a four-part version without a version word (`Node 2.4.1.7`); a letters-only key made of three-letter words joined by `_` or `-` is a phrase and stays; `Class @ Marriott.Library` is an email (0281). Known leaks the exemptions allow: a phone number written `801/555 1234` is masked in full because `801/555` is not an M/D date, but `12/31 5551234` keeps `12/31` and masks the rest, and a course-number-shaped secret after a word of letters (`mail 4155-555`) stays. `Release 192.168.1.20` stays because of the version word. The digit rule's other behaviour (6+ digits, ISO dates, `(801) 555 1234`) is unchanged.
Where: `src/command_center/core/redact.py` (`_EXEMPT`, `_redact_body`, `_DIGIT_GROUP`, `_VERSION_WORD`, `_redact_ipv4`, `_is_phrase`, `_is_token_run`, `_mask_code`), `tests/test_redact_agenda.py`, `docs/contracts/core.md` (Redaction).
Revisit when: A real agenda line is damaged by a shape not listed (add the shape with a boundary and a test), a secret is seen that one of these exemptions let through, or `redact.py` is split (it is over the 300-line aim).
